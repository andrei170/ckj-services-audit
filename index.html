Your Question, Answered First
“Can you actually help me win projects like these?”

You said at booking you were unsure whether we could help you land the large contractor projects — you have around eight in the pipeline to January, and the schemes themselves run to very large values. It is the right question and it deserves a straight answer rather than a pitch.

What will not work for you: consumer lead generation. Facebook and Instagram ads do not reach a main contractor's procurement team, and anyone selling you a homeowner funnel for commercial scaffolding is selling you the wrong thing. We are not going to.

What does apply: main contractors and their QS teams do search, and they do shortlist. When a project manager needs a NASC-accredited scaffolder for a data centre fit-out, they search exactly that. You have already written the page that should win it. What is missing is everything around it — no analytics to prove which page produced which enquiry, a homepage that undermines the sector pages, and no tender-ready credential set in one place.

So the honest answer is: not by sending you leads. By making the pages you already built findable, credible and measurable. That is a smaller, cheaper job than what you were probably expecting to be sold.

Finding 1 — The Lorem Ipsum Is Still There
Flagged in August. Re-read today. Unchanged.

On the homepage, under the heading WHY CHOOSE US, the copy reads:

WHY CHOOSE US Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit. Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit. Nullam ut commodo nisl, ...

It sits directly beneath four genuinely good capability lines — structure-aware planning, rapid mobilisation, compliance and food safety. So the section that exists specifically to answer "why you" answers it in Latin.

Why this matters more for you than for most businesses: your buyer is a procurement professional. They are assessing whether you are organised enough to be trusted on a live site with programme dates and safety obligations. Placeholder text on the homepage is the single cheapest reason to be dropped from a shortlist, and it is entirely within your control.

It is twenty minutes of writing. It is the first thing to fix, ahead of everything else on this page.

Finding 2 — The Tag Manager Is Installed And Empty
Exactly the same pattern as the homepage — set up, not finished

Container GTM-PBCF2D7P loads on every page. There is no GA4 property behind it, no conversion tracking, no call tracking, no Google Ads tag.

GTM-PBCF2D7P     present on every page GA4                not found Google Ads tag   not found LocalBusiness    not found

For a business whose enquiries arrive in small numbers and are worth a great deal each, this is more costly than it is for a consumer trade. You cannot afford to not know which of your four sector pages produced the enquiry that became a contract. One data centre page outperforming the other three would change where you point everything — and right now that information is simply not being collected.

Finding 3 — Three Pieces Of Housekeeping That Read As Carelessness
Small individually. Cumulative to a procurement reader.
  • A live “Coming Soon” page. /coming-soon/ is published, indexed in your sitemap, and reads “WEBSITE COMING SOON” with your email and mobile. Your website is not coming soon — it is here and it is good. This page should be deleted or redirected.
  • The default blog post was never removed. Your only post is “Hello world!”, dated 19 May 2025 — the post WordPress creates on installation. It is in your sitemap.
  • The www version of your .com domain is down. www.ckjscaffoldingservices.com returns HTTP 522 — a server error — while the version without www loads fine and the .co.uk loads fine. Anyone who types or is sent the www address of your .com gets an error page. ⚠️ Worth checking which address is printed on your vans, signage and tender documents.

None of these loses you a project on its own. Together, on a shortlist where someone is looking for a reason to cut the list from six to three, they are the kind of thing that does.

Finding 4 — The Case Studies Are The Weakest Link In The Strongest Chain
400 words, for the audience that cares most

You have a case studies page and it runs to about 400 words — against sector pages of roughly a thousand each. For a consumer trade that would be fine. For contractor work it is the wrong way round.

A QS shortlisting scaffolders wants: the project type, the scale, the programme, what the constraint was, how you handled live operations or restricted access, and who the main contractor was. That is the document that wins a place on a tender list, and it is currently the thinnest page on an otherwise substantial site.

You mentioned around eight projects running to January. Each one is a case study you are already being paid to produce.

What Is Genuinely Good
The hard part is already done, and that is unusual
  • Four sector pages at roughly a thousand words each — warehouse (961), data centre (1,016), distribution centre (1,057), food distribution (1,065). Specific, technical, and aimed squarely at a commercial buyer rather than a homeowner. This is the content most firms never get round to writing.
  • The positioning is clear and consistent: NASC-accredited, commercial, programme-led, safety-first, nationwide. There is no consumer/commercial confusion anywhere, which is rarer than it should be.
  • Titles and meta descriptions are written for the right reader — "Data Centre Scaffolding Contractors", "Distribution Centre Scaffolding" — not generic.
  • Food distribution as a separate sector page is a genuinely smart call. It is a specific compliance environment and almost nobody writes a dedicated page for it.

This is not a case for a new website. It is four small jobs on a site that is already most of the way there.

The Order We Would Do It In
  • 1. Replace the Lorem Ipsum. Twenty minutes. Nothing else on this list matters while it is live.
  • 2. Put GA4 into the container that is already there, with conversion tracking on the enquiry routes, so you can see which sector page produces contracts.
  • 3. Delete or redirect /coming-soon/ and the "Hello world!" post.
  • 4. Fix the www record on the .com, and check which address is on your vans and tender documents.
  • 5. Rebuild the case studies page — one page per project, with scale, programme, constraint and outcome.
  • 6. Add Organization and LocalBusiness structured data.
  • 7. Only then look at paid search, and only on procurement-intent terms. ⛔ Not consumer social.

Four thousand words aimed at the right buyer, hanging off a homepage that says Lorem Ipsum.

The gap between what you have built and what it is doing for you is almost entirely housekeeping.
