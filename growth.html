How You Get Onto A Main Contractor's List When They Have Never Heard Of You

Prepared for Kris Perry · 2 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
Read This Before Anything Else
This is not what we are proposing today

This page is the longer game, written down so you can see it exists. We are not pitching it, not pricing it, and we would not start it until the tracking and the website housekeeping are done and you have seen whether we deliver.

It is here because you asked a fair question — whether we could actually help you win the bigger projects — and “no, but here is what would” is a more useful answer than a pitch.

⚠️ It is also slow. Supplier approval runs in quarters, not weeks. Anyone promising you new contractors inside a month does not understand how your industry buys.

First, What Does Not Work
ApproachWhy not, for you
Facebook or Instagram adsA contracts manager at a main contractor is not choosing a scaffolder from a social feed. Wrong audience, wrong mindset, wrong decision process entirely.
Google Ads at volumeSome narrow value on specialist terms, but the search volume for "data centre scaffolding contractor" is tiny. It is a trickle, not a channel.
Cold email blastsYour target list is small and the people on it are known quantities. Burning them with a sequence costs you the one approach you get. ⛔ It also risks the reputation of kris@, which is your only inbound route since the site has no form.
Cold calling a switchboardProcurement teams at Tier 1 contractors are gatekept hard. Ringing the main number gets you nowhere, repeatedly.
All four fail for the same reason. They are volume instruments, and this is not a volume problem. There are perhaps a few hundred companies in the UK who could ever give you work. You do not advertise at a few hundred named companies. You go and get registered with them.
The Actual Mechanism — Supplier Portals
This is the front door, and it is free

Most Tier 1 and Tier 2 main contractors run a supplier or subcontractor registration portal. You create an account, upload your accreditations, insurance certificates, health and safety record and capability statement, and you are in their system.

That is where their procurement team actually looks when a scaffolding package needs pricing. Not Google, not LinkedIn, not a feed. Their own approved list.

⭐ Being registered costs nothing but the paperwork. The reason most firms your size are not on these lists is not cost — it is that nobody has ever sat down and worked through them methodically.

The Three Steps, In Order
  • 1. Register. Work systematically through every main contractor active in your sectors and regions. Account created, documents uploaded, pre-qualification completed. Unglamorous and it is the whole foundation.
  • 2. Introduce. A short, specific approach to a named contracts manager, supply chain lead or bid manager — not an address like procurement@. We are registered, here is what we do, here is a job like yours we have finished, please consider us for the next package in this sector.
  • 3. Follow up by phone. A fortnight later, to a named person who has already had something from you. That is a different call from a cold one, and construction is still a phone industry.
That is account-based business development, not outreach. A hundred or two hundred companies, worked properly over months, with a record of every touch. ⭐ Which is also why the quote tracker comes first — without somewhere to hold it, this is unmanageable by the third week.
What You Would Need Before Step Two

In construction the thing that opens a door is not a brochure or a download. It is a set of documents procurement already expects to receive. These are useful immediately, whether or not you ever do any of the above — they feed straight into the tenders you are already answering.

DocumentWhat it is and why it matters
⭐ Capability statementThe single most important one. Company overview, NASC and other accreditations, insurance levels, health and safety record, plant and kit list, sectors, key projects with values and programme, references. Every supply chain team asks for one. Most firms your size either have a poor one or none.
Four sector case studiesOne each for warehouse, distribution centre, data centre and food distribution — matching the four pages you already publish. Written the way a quantity surveyor reads: scale, programme, the constraint, how it was handled, the outcome. Your case studies page is currently 400 words, the thinnest page on your site.
PQQ answer bankThe same twenty or so questions appear on every pre-qualification form. Written once, properly, they stop being a day's work each time.
Health, safety and compliance packRAMS templates, method statements, training matrix, inspection regime. Procurement checks this before anything else, and a tidy one signals an organised firm before a word is read.
A scope checklist for QSsThe one genuinely useful thing you could give the other side: what to specify in a scaffolding package so the prices they get back are comparable. ⭐ It makes their job easier, which is rare, and it positions you as the one who knows.
You are already being paid to produce the case studies

You mentioned around eight projects running through to January. Each one is a case study, photographed and written up as it happens rather than reconstructed from memory two years later.

Nine Campaigns, Ordered By What They Return

⭐ The first four cost almost nothing and do not need us.

⭐ 1 — The portal sweep

Work through every main contractor operating in warehousing, distribution, data centres and food logistics across your regions. Register with each. Free, systematic, and it is the foundation everything else stands on.

Nothing else on this list works until this is done. An introduction to a contractor you are not registered with just creates work for them.

⭐ 2 — The site next door

When you are on a scheme, the main contractor is already there and already knows your work. Other packages on the same site, and their other sites in the region, need scaffolding too.

The warmest approach available to you, and it costs a conversation. It gets forgotten because nobody tracks which contractor is on which site when — which the quote tracker fixes.

⭐ 3 — Lapsed contractor reactivation

Every contractor you have quoted or worked for and not heard from in eighteen months. They know you, you are already approved, and nothing needs re-establishing. Free, warm, and currently invisible because the history lives in your head.

⭐ 4 — Be in the inbox at review time

Approved supplier lists get reviewed periodically. Being in front of someone the month they are reviewing beats being in front of them ten times when they are not. Ask each contractor when their review happens and diarise it. Almost nobody does this.

5 — Data centre specialisation

Data centre construction is one of the few parts of UK construction with real momentum, and the scaffolding is genuinely specialist — clean conditions, phased handovers, restricted access, live plant. You already publish a 1,016-word data centre page, which is more than most firms have.

Target the main contractors building them specifically, and lead with that page rather than a general introduction. ⚠️ Honest caveat: it is a competitive sector precisely because it is busy.

6 — Cold store and food distribution

Your 1,065-word food distribution page is the single most unusual asset you have. Almost nobody writes a dedicated page for it, because it is a specific compliance environment most scaffolders would rather avoid.

That is exactly why it is worth leading with. Fewer competitors, a buyer with a real problem, and you have already done the work of looking like a specialist.

7 — LinkedIn, but not ads

Contracts managers and QSs are genuinely on LinkedIn. Not for advertising — for being visible. You posting a finished birdcage, a temporary roof going up, a job handed over on programme.

It costs nothing, it compounds, and it means when your name reaches someone they can see you are real before they reply. ⚠️ It needs doing consistently or not at all.

8 — Credibility where procurement checks

⭐ You are better placed here than you may realise. Your About page states you hold Constructionline Gold and are audited members of the Scaffolding Association, and that you are working towards CHAS Elite. Constructionline Gold in particular is a credential a lot of main contractors screen on, and plenty of firms your size do not have it.

So the gap is not the credentials. It is that almost nobody can see them. They sit in a paragraph near the foot of one page. They should be on the homepage, in the capability statement, and attached to every approach.

🔴 Before any of that, settle the NASC question — see the audit. Leading with a credential that cannot be verified is worse than leading with none, and procurement checks this first.

9 — The other trades on your sites

Groundworks, steel erectors, cladding and roofing contractors work the same schemes and often know a scaffolding package is coming before procurement advertises it. A handful of good relationships there is worth more than any list. Slow, entirely relationship-based, and not something an agency can do for you.
