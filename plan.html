Plan Of Action · CKJ Services Limited · Harwich, Essex

Four Weeks, And Nothing That Asks You To Work Differently

Prepared for Kris Perry · 2 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
The Shape Of It
Two things, in this order, and a third only if you want it

One — somewhere every quote lives, that tells you when one has gone quiet. That is the part worth your money and it is the part that pays for itself.

Two — the housekeeping on your website. Cheap, quick, and it stops you looking disorganised to someone deciding whether to put you on a list. It is not marketing and we will not pretend it is.

Three — going after contractors you have never worked with. Real, slow, and written up separately. We would not start it until the first two are done and you have seen whether we deliver.

⛔ Nothing here changes how enquiries reach you. They still arrive by phone and to kris@, exactly as they do now. Nobody is asking you to fill in forms or work off a different system on site.

Week One — The Housekeeping

All of this is a morning's work and none of it needs anything from you except a login.

#WhatWhy it is first
1Replace the Lorem Ipsum on your homepageIt sits under “Why Choose Us” — the one place a contractor looks to decide whether you are worth shortlisting. Twenty minutes.
2Delete the live “Coming Soon” pageIt is published and in your sitemap, telling anyone who finds it that your website is not ready. It is ready.
3Remove the “Hello world!” postThe default WordPress post from May 2025, still your only blog entry, still indexed.
4Fix the www version of the .comIt returns a server error while the other versions load. ⚠️ Worth checking which address is on your vans, signage and tender documents.
5Put GA4 behind the tag manager you already haveContainer GTM-PBCF2D7P loads on every page with nothing behind it. Someone fitted the pipe and never connected it.
6Conversion tracking on your enquiry routesSo a phone tap and an email click register as something, not just traffic.
7Add Organization and LocalBusiness structured dataMissing entirely. It is how search engines understand what the business is.
What week one actually buys you

Nothing you will feel immediately — and we would rather say that than dress it up. What it buys is the ability to answer a question nobody can answer today: have your four sector pages ever produced an enquiry? In a quarter you will know. If the answer is no, you stop thinking about your website entirely and we both save time.

Weeks One And Two — The Quote Tracker
StepWhat happensFrom you
1Agree your stages. Enquiry, site visit, estimating, quote out, chasing, won, lost — renamed to whatever you actually say. If you call it pricing, it says pricing.15 minutes on a call
2Agree the fields on a quote. Contractor, contact, project, sector, scaffold type, value, programme, date sent, decision expected, who estimated it, and how long it took.15 minutes
3Load your history. Every quote from the last couple of years — won, lost and gone quiet. This is the one part we cannot do without you, and it is what makes the system useful on day one instead of month six.⚠️ An export, or a spreadsheet, or whatever you have
4Connect kris@ so enquiries become records automatically, and set up call logging.A login
5Set the chase timings. We would start at 30, 60 and 90 days rather than 7 or 14, because a tender on a large scheme is not a roofing quote. It can vary by value too.A decision
6Walk through it with whoever will use it. Half an hour, screen shared, with somebody at a keyboard doing it rather than watching.30 minutes
⚠️ The honest risk, said out loud

A system nobody updates is worse than a notebook, because it looks authoritative while being wrong.

If you are the only person who will ever open it, say so now and we will build it around that — fewer fields, more automation, nothing that relies on somebody remembering. The commonest way this fails is not the software. It is building for a team that does not exist.

Weeks Three And Four — Make It Earn Its Place
  • Work the back catalogue. Every quote loaded in step 3 that went quiet and was never closed out. Some are dead. Some are not. This is where the system pays for itself, in the first fortnight, out of work you have already done.
  • First real numbers. What is actually out there, what it is worth, and your genuine win rate rather than a feeling.
  • Which contractors are worth your estimating time. If one has sent you nine tenders and you have won none, that is weeks a year of unpaid estimating. You cannot see it today.
  • Which of your four sectors converts. You publish a page for each. One of them is probably carrying the others.
  • Tune the chase timings. Thirty days may be too keen or too slow. It is a number in a box, not a rebuild.
After That
OptionWhen it makes sense
Case studies and a capability statementIf you are answering pre-qualification forms regularly. You have around eight projects running to January — each one is a case study you are already being paid to produce. Detail here.
Going after new contractorsOnly if your honest answer to “how do you reach a contractor you have never worked with” is “I do not”. It runs in quarters, not weeks.
Hiring⭐ If the real constraint is that you could not crew more work even if it arrived. In that case everything above is the wrong priority and we would say so.
Paid advertisingProbably never, and certainly not now. Your buyers do not find scaffolders in a feed, and we are not going to sell you something we do not think works.
What We Need From You
  • 1. Your quote history — whatever form it is in. Messy is fine. This is the only genuinely blocking item.
  • 2. An hour, in total, spread across three short calls to agree stages, fields and timings.
  • 3. Logins for the website, the tag manager and the kris@ mailbox.
  • 4. An honest answer on who will use it. If it is only you, that changes what we build.

Four weeks, one hour of your time, and no change to how you work.

The only thing we cannot do without you is the old quotes.
