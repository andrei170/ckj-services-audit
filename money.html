One Quote You Would Otherwise Have Forgotten Pays For All Of It

Prepared for Kris Perry · 2 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
Read This Bit First
We have deliberately not filled in your numbers

Every figure on this page is blank or illustrative, because we do not know your contract values, your quote volume or your margin, and we are not going to invent them to make a case look good.

Fill them in on the call and the arithmetic does itself. If the answer is that this does not pay for itself, that is a real answer and we would rather you reached it in twenty minutes than in six months.

The Four Numbers That Decide It
 What we needYour figure

DOf the ones you never hear back on, how many do you actively chase?—
D is the one that matters, and it is the one almost nobody can answer. Not because people are careless — because there is currently nothing recording it. You know the ones you remember. The question is how many there were altogether.
Why This Arithmetic Is Different For You
The asymmetry is the whole argument

A roofer who forgets to chase a quote loses a few hundred pounds. Annoying, survivable, and it happens every week without anybody noticing.

You are quoting work on schemes that run into the hundreds of millions. A single scaffolding package on one of those is not a few hundred pounds. So the cost of one forgotten follow-up is not a rounding error for you — it is potentially the largest avoidable loss in your year.

And the frequency works against you too. A roofer sends hundreds of quotes a year, so the law of averages protects them. You send a small number of large ones. Lose one and there is no volume to absorb it.

That combination — few quotes, enormous values, long decision windows — is the strongest case for tracking that exists in any trade. It is also why almost every firm your size eventually buys one.

The Honest Version Of That Argument
Where it would not pay off

We would rather say this than have you discover it later. This does not pay for itself if:

  • You already chase everything reliably. If no quote of yours has ever gone quiet and been forgotten, the recovery argument is worth nothing and you should not buy it for that reason.
  • Nobody will use it. A system that is not updated is worse than a notebook, because it looks authoritative while being wrong. If you are the only person who would ever open it, the honest answer is that adoption is the risk, not the software.
  • Your work is genuinely all repeat relationships. If every job comes from four contractors you speak to weekly, tracking adds less than it would for someone chasing cold tenders.

None of those stop the second argument below, which stands on its own.

The Second Thing It Buys, Which Is Not About Recovery
You currently cannot answer any of these
  • What is your actual win rate? Not a feeling — a number.
  • Which sector wins most? You have four sector pages. Do data centres convert better than warehousing? Worth knowing before you decide where to point everything.
  • Which contractors are worth your estimating time? If one has put ten tenders your way and you have won none, that is costing you weeks of unpaid estimating a year.
  • What is sitting out there right now, in total? The number you would want if a bank, an insurer or a buyer ever asked.
  • Where did the enquiry come from? Right now Tag Manager is installed on your site with no analytics behind it, so nobody can tell you whether a single enquiry has ever come from your sector pages.

None of those are recoverable money. All of them are decisions you are currently making blind. For a business whose estimating time is its scarcest resource, knowing which contractors waste it is worth something on its own.

The Cheapest Thing On This Page Is Not The System
Do these whether you work with us or not
  • Replace the Lorem Ipsum on your homepage. It sits under “Why Choose Us”. Twenty minutes, free, and it is in front of every contractor who looks you up.
  • Delete the live “Coming Soon” page and the “Hello world!” post from May 2025. Both are published and in your sitemap.
  • Fix the www version of your .com — it returns a server error while the other versions work. Check which address is on your vans and tender documents.
  • Put analytics behind the tag manager you already have. One afternoon, and it ends every future argument about what works.

We are telling you these for nothing because they are the four things most likely to be costing you a place on a shortlist, and none of them needs us.

Few quotes, large values, long decisions.

That is the shape of business where one forgotten follow-up costs more than the system does.
